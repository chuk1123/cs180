Becoming Friends with Your Camera

Kevin Chu · CS180 · Fall 2026

Overview

This project explores how perspective changes with camera position. For the first two comparisons, I used zoom to match the framing after moving the camera; the visible difference comes from the change in position.

Part 1: Selfie — the wrong way vs. the right way

In the close selfie, my nose is much closer to the camera than the rest of my face, so it appears disproportionately large. After stepping back, the relative depth across my face is smaller. Zooming restores the framing but does not restore the close-up perspective distortion.

Part 2: Architectural perspective compression

From farther away, the front and receding portions of Bancroft Library are at more similar distances from the camera, so the facade appears flatter. Moving closer enlarges those distance differences, making the receding lines and depth more noticeable.

Part 3: Dolly zoom

I placed a water bottle on a campus balustrade, then moved the camera back while zooming in to keep the bottle at nearly the same size in the frame. As the background is magnified more strongly, its apparent depth changes to create the dolly-zoom effect.
