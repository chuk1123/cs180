Colorizing the Prokudin-Gorskii Photo Collection

01

Approach

Each glass plate stores three grayscale exposures in B, G, R order. I split it into thirds, kept blue fixed, and shifted green and red until they matched blue.

I used normalized cross-correlation (NCC) to score each shift. First, subtract each image’s average intensity. Then add the products of corresponding pixels and divide by the product of the images’ L2 norms. A larger score means the patterns agree better. Mean subtraction and normalization reduce sensitivity to overall brightness differences.

The alternative L2 score adds squared pixel differences; smaller is better. My implementation omits the square root because it does not change which shift wins. All displayed results use NCC on the original channel intensities.

I scored only interior pixels, excluding a border proportional to image size (10% of the shorter dimension). This prevents scan borders from dominating the score. The crop also covers wrapped pixels from np.roll; refinement adds the estimated displacement and local search radius to the border margin. Saved images remain uncropped.

For single-scale alignment, two loops test every horizontal and vertical shift from −15 to 15 pixels, and keep the shift with the highest NCC score. The winning shifts are applied to the original channels, which are stacked in R, G, B order.

For large TIFFs, I recursively halve both images with anti-aliased resizing until either dimension is below 200 pixels. At that smallest level, I search ±15 pixels. Moving back toward full resolution, I double the estimated offset, shift the channel, and search ±2 pixels for a correction. Adding each correction gives the final full-resolution displacement. This avoids a huge exhaustive search on the original TIFF.

05

Fixing Emir with a different reference

Direct red-to-blue NCC failed on Emir. His blue robe has very different brightness in the red and blue exposures, so raw-pixel similarity selected an incorrect shift. I tried aligning red to green first, then moving the aligned red and green together into blue’s coordinate system.

I used the same pyramid and NCC metric: estimate red’s shift relative to the original green, estimate green’s shift relative to blue, then add those offsets to obtain red’s final shift relative to blue. Red and green matched better for this image. The resulting offsets are still reported relative to blue.

This reference change removes the large double image. It was tested as an additional experiment on Emir; the gallery above retains the required direct-to-blue baseline. Colored outer strips come from scan borders and wrapped pixels. I did not implement automatic cropping or claim this experiment as the “better features” extension.
